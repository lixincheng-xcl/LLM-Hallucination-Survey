# Mitigation-case primary-text audit

Verified on 5 October 2026 against the formal proceedings PDFs. This file accompanies `mitigation_cases.csv` and implements the pre-recorded `codebook.md`; it does not redefine the cohort. The eight cases here form the mitigation subset of the thirteen named studies in frozen release `ca6e155`, Appendix Table 8. This is a purposive mechanism-diversity audit, not a systematic search, field-wide reporting-prevalence estimate, meta-analysis, or new model experiment. Coding and checking are AI-assisted, not independent human double annotation. Final author verification remains necessary.

## Source and locator conventions

All page numbers below are **one-based PDF pages**, not journal/proceedings running pages. Formal version, primary landing page, PDF URL, SHA-256 and exact inspected scope are in the CSV. Downloaded source PDFs and extracted text are kept outside the repository, only at `local_cache/acl_submission_sources`; `mitigation_source_manifest.json` there records download digests and page counts. No claim is made to have read every reference entry or every qualitative example in the longer appendices. The inspected methods, experiments and relevant appendices are specified case by case.

The descriptions below distinguish a paper's observation from this audit's interpretation. “Not located” refers only to the named inspected sections. A task-quality proxy, generated-fact count, or binary informativeness label is not silently promoted to required-aspect completeness or a matched answered-question coverage control. Different model names/prompts do not establish independent errors. “Related component analysis” records useful but narrower evidence; it is not a criticism for failing to perform an experiment outside the paper's stated purpose.

## 1. FactAlign

Primary source: [Findings EMNLP 2024 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-emnlp.955/). Read Sections 3–6 and Limitations, PDF pp.3–9; Appendix A prompts and B example, pp.12–13.

- **Mechanism and target.** Section 3.1 (p.3) defines atomic factuality by support from a specified corpus C. Section 4.1 (p.4) decomposes responses, resolves context, generates queries, retrieves evidence and assesses statements. Section 4.2 (pp.4–5) combines response KTO with sentence fKTO; these need binary accept/reject labels rather than DPO's preferred/dispreferred response pairs. Section 4.3 (p.5) refreshes generated training responses across iterations. The target is world factuality operationalized through corpus support, not a guarantee of truth beyond that corpus.
- **Information safeguard.** Section 3.1 explicitly addresses precision-only gaming. Its *printed* recall definition is `min(1, |A|/K)`, where A contains generated atomic statements; do not silently rewrite the numerator as supported statements. Table 1 (p.7) reports precision, claim count and f1@100, plus MT-Bench helpfulness. This monitors output quantity and a separate utility proxy, not coverage of a reference list of required aspects. Section 6.3/Figure 3 (pp.8–9) studies precision/recall trade-offs through data mixing.
- **Evaluation relationship.** Sections 5.2/5.4 (p.6) specify GPT-3.5-turbo, Wikipedia, ColBERT retrieval and a modified SAFE-style evaluator for both alignment construction and LongFact assessment. Preliminary correlations with SAFE are 0.93 for supported counts and 0.82 for unsupported counts; these are agreement evidence, not a new human truth audit. FActScore and GPT-4 MT-Bench provide additional evaluations with distinct targets. Limitations (p.9) acknowledge automatic-label/evaluation errors and corpus/retrieval dependence.
- **Controlled evidence.** Table 1 (p.7) shows Gemma-2B-SFT versus FactAlign: LongFact precision 77.41→79.59, claim count 66.8→135.1, and f1@100 61.74→86.51. This is evidence that quantity changed along with factual precision under this protocol. For the sentence-loss ablation, Table 2/Section 6.1 (pp.7–8) compares the full **one-iteration** result 77.10 against 73.12 without fKTO; comparing 86.51 from three iterations with 73.12 would confound the ablation with iteration count.
- **Resources and joint validation.** Section 5.5 (p.6) gives four V100 GPUs, approximately 1–2 hours per training run and an estimated USD25 per evaluation run. Table 2 is a training-component ablation. We code **related component analysis**: final scorer-based gains and ablations are tested, but fixed-scorer validity across the intervention against independently adjudicated truth labels was not located in the inspected scope. Training cost is not inference latency.

## 2. Self-RAG

Primary source: [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/25f7be9694d7b32d5cc670927b8091e1-Abstract-Conference.html). Read Sections 3–5, pp.3–9; Appendices A.1–A.3, B and C.1–C.2, pp.16–21.

- **Mechanism and unit.** Table 1/Sections 3.1–3.3 (pp.3–6) distinguish retrieval need, passage relevance, segment support and response utility. A critic learns GPT-4 feedback to annotate training data; the deployed generator itself emits reflection tokens and ranks candidate segments with their probabilities. The separately trained critic is not needed at inference. Thus, externally retrieved evidence is combined with a learned self-evaluation signal, not an independently operating verifier.
- **Information safeguard.** Section 4.1/Table 2 (pp.6/8) jointly reports ASQA correctness, ROUGE, MAUVE and citation scores; other tasks use QA labels and biography FActScore. Appendix B.3 (p.20) fixes ASQA maximum generation to 100 tokens across baselines to reduce length bias. This length cap is a genuine safeguard but does not match actual information or all final lengths; biography generation uses different overall/segment budgets. Section 5.2/Figure 3(b) (p.9) shows stronger support weighting trades fluency for citation precision. Citation recall measures evidence support for generated claims, not coverage of everything the question requires.
- **Human and model assessment.** Appendix A.1 (pp.16–17) has authors inspect 20 GPT-4 annotations per aspect. Appendix C.1 (p.20) evaluates critic agreement with GPT-4, which remains agreement with training-label provenance. Appendix C.2 (p.21) assesses 50 sampled PopQA/Bio outputs for support/plausibility and whether predicted ISREL/ISSUP tokens match human inspections. Crucially, the **S&P calculation excludes instances the model predicts irrelevant or no support**. This is not an unselected population-wide support rate; the passage does not justify inventing annotator independence or inter-annotator agreement.
- **Resources.** Section 4.3 (p.7), Appendix B.1 (pp.19–20) and Figure 3(c) (p.9) provide beam/threshold settings, training and inference hardware, and retrieval frequency. Four A100-80GB GPUs train the models; one or two Quadro RTX6000-24GB GPUs support inference. Retrieval frequency is a cost-related control, not measured wall-clock latency.
- **Joint code.** **Explicit joint generator/intervention/detector test, narrowly scoped**: C.2 checks generated outputs and their reflection labels together. The support-weight ablation links the selection signal to output quality. Neither establishes a full matrix of before/after detector calibration, model shifts and information-matched generation. The conditional S&P denominator must accompany any account of this human audit.

## 3. CAD

Primary source: [NAACL 2024 short paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-short.69/). Read Sections 2–5/Limitations, pp.2–6, and Appendix A/Table 4, p.9.

- **Mechanism and access.** Section 2 (p.2) contrasts context-conditioned and context-free next-token distributions. Its combined logit is `(1+alpha)*context_logit - alpha*query_only_logit`. It changes reliance on supplied context without additional training or an explicit factual checker. Both distributions require logit access; the intervention is not usable through generation-only access under the described implementation.
- **Reference frame.** Section 3.1 (pp.2–3) evaluates summarization source consistency, NQ-Swap knowledge conflict and MemoTrap instruction-following. NQ-Swap deliberately changes entities in supporting text and evaluates the modified answer. Therefore, better source adherence can require departing from world knowledge. This case motivates separating **source faithfulness** from **world factuality** rather than relabeling all gains as truth gains.
- **Quality and evaluation.** Tables 2–3 (p.4) pair summary FactKB/BERT-precision with ROUGE-L; QA uses exact match. ROUGE-L is a task-quality safeguard, not a complete-content annotation. FactKB and BERT-based metrics are evaluators, not internal CAD components. Their distinct model identities do not alone validate them on the output distribution induced by CAD.
- **Resources and joint code.** Section 2.2 (p.2) specifies paired computations; Section 3.2 (p.3) controls alpha/decoding; Limitations (p.6) identifies access and task-dependent tuning. Measured latency was not located in Sections 2–5, Limitations or Appendix A. Do not infer an exact twofold wall-clock slowdown. The alpha/model-size analyses (Section 5, pp.4–5) are **related component analysis**, not a separate detector calibration study.

## 4. DoLa

Primary source: [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/edc36117f795ca52a0cbf6a7b3882859-Abstract-Conference.html). Read Sections 2–4/Conclusion, pp.3–9, and Appendices D–F, pp.16–18.

- **Mechanism.** Section 2 (pp.3–5) selects a premature layer by maximal Jensen–Shannon divergence from the final-layer distribution within a candidate bucket, then contrasts token probabilities with a plausibility restriction. This requires internal representations/vocabulary projection; it does not retrieve knowledge or train a second model. Section 3.2 (pp.5–6) describes validation for candidate buckets; selection is not a universally fixed layer rule.
- **Information evidence.** Table 1 (p.6) reports Truth, Info, the joint metric and Reject. In LLaMA-33B, baseline→DoLa gives Truth **62.5→56.4**, Info **69.0→92.4**, joint score **31.7→49.1**, and Reject **38.1→8.2**. Thus a larger joint score cannot be described as an improvement in the raw truth component for every model. Section 4.3 (p.8) includes a counterexample in which abstention becomes an informative but wrong answer. Appendix D/Table 8 (pp.16–17) evaluates grammar/cohesiveness with GPT-4 while explicitly excluding factuality. These are informative/refusal safeguards, not required-aspect coverage or matched-risk selective prediction.
- **Evaluation provenance.** Sections 3.1–3.3 (pp.5–7) use fine-tuned GPT-3 truth/info classifiers and task labels. Reasoning answer accuracy is not the same construct as long-form factuality. A separate final evaluator is not evidence that evaluator errors are independent of generation.
- **Measured resources.** Section 4.2/Table 2 (p.8) measures latency ratios **1.01–1.08** and throughput. Appendix E/Table 9 (pp.16–17) adds memory; Appendix F (pp.17–18) specifies V100 GPUs, batch one, fp16, 817 TruthfulQA prompts and forced 50-token generation without stopping. This is genuine measured resource evidence. It must not be coded as merely qualitative or generalized to another batching/hardware/serving regime.
- **Joint code.** Dynamic/static layer ablations and downstream metrics are **related component analysis**. A fixed-detector reliability test before/after the intervention was not located in the inspected sections. Conclusion (p.9) limits the method to knowledge available to the model.

## 5. TruthX

Primary source: [ACL 2024 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.483/). Read Sections 3–5, Conclusion/Limitations, pp.3–10; Appendix A/B material, pp.13–14; Appendix C evaluator details, p.16.

- **Mechanism.** Sections 3.1–3.3 (pp.3–5) use truthful/untruthful QA pairs to learn semantic and truthful latent spaces with reconstruction, contrastive and editing losses. Latent directions edit attention/FFN representations; validation classification accuracy selects modules. Appendix A (p.13) specifies auxiliary-network dimensions, optimizer and the split of each training fold into training/validation. This is trained representation editing, not parameter-free decoding or an external fact checker.
- **Information control and results.** Table 1/Section 4.3 (p.6) report Truth, Info, their joint score and explicit no-comment behavior. Llama-2-7B-chat Truth rises **36.96→72.95**, Info **86.29→89.72**, and joint **31.90→65.45** in this protocol. Section 5.2/Figure 4 (pp.7–8) adds perplexity from the original Llama-2 model. Appendix B.2/Table 7 (pp.13–14) shows that latent dimension affects abstention; this supports discussing usefulness, but binary Info and perplexity do not establish all required content is retained.
- **Evaluation and transfer.** Section 4.2 (p.5) uses two-fold TruthfulQA splits, so train/test questions are separated. Appendix C (p.16) describes fine-tuned GPT-3 truth/info evaluation. Section 4.1/Table 2 (pp.5–6) turns transfer datasets into closed-book multiple choice. Section 5.4 (p.9) tests compatible-dimensional cross-model transfer. None of these should be rewritten as universally calibrated free-generation factuality.
- **Resources and joint code.** Auxiliary model/training configuration is reported (Appendix A, p.13); measured inference or training wall-clock time was not located in the inspected Sections 3–5/Appendix A–B. The internal classification probe, loss/direction ablations and final truth scores are **related component analysis**, not an independent truth detector validated after editing. Limitations (p.10) state that truth is not guaranteed and missing knowledge is not injected.

## 6. CoVe

Primary source: [Findings ACL 2024 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.212/). Read Sections 3–4/Limitations, pp.3–9, and Appendix Sections 6–8/Tables 4–6, pp.12–13.

- **Mechanism and reuse.** Sections 3.1–3.4 (pp.3–4) draft an answer, plan verification questions, answer them and revise. Joint, two-step, factored and factor+revise differ in access to the initial answer and decomposition. The principal LLaMA-65B experiment uses the same model across stages and no retrieved evidence within correction. Hiding the draft removes that direct conditioning path, but it is not a demonstration of independent knowledge errors.
- **Information safeguards.** Tables 1–2 (p.6) report positive/negative entities and multi-span precision, recall and F1. Table 3 (p.7) gives a particularly useful same-generator trade-off: few-shot baseline→factor+revise improves biography FActScore **55.9→71.4** while fact count declines **16.6→12.3**. This shows why factual precision and quantity need separate denominators. It does not prove either that every removed fact was useful or that deletion explains the whole improvement. The ten-sentence clipping test in Section 4.3 (p.8) is for a **different Llama-2-70B-chat baseline** and must not be presented as a matched-length control for the LLaMA-65B CoVe comparison.
- **Evaluation and joint analysis.** Section 4.1 (p.5) uses gold entity/QA answers and an external retrieval-based FActScore implementation for biographies. Section 4.3 (p.8) compares verification-question correctness and final answers; Appendix Section 6/Table 4 (p.12) varies verification formats, and Section 8/Table 6 (p.13) evaluates SelfCheckGPT filtering thresholds. We code **related component analysis**. These useful checks do not establish fixed-detector calibration across pre/post revision distributions at equal information.
- **Resources.** Appendix Section 7/Table 5 (p.12) derives worst-case factor+revise calls **1+s+2f** (s sentences, f verification questions), and discusses parallelization. Limitations (p.9) note additional tokens and closed-book knowledge limits. Call count is an explicit resource account, not measured latency or an equal-token-budget comparison.

## 7. CRITIC

Primary source: [ICLR 2024 proceedings](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/fef126561bbf9d4467dbb8d27334b8fe-Abstract-Conference.html). Read Sections 3–4, pp.3–9; Appendix A p.21, C.3 p.23, D.1–D.5 pp.24–27.

- **Mechanism and protocol.** Section 3/Algorithm 1 (pp.3–5) iterates tool interaction, critique and correction. Section 4.1 (pp.5–6) uses web search for free-form QA with up to seven tool interactions and three correction iterations. CRITIC processes generated answers; **CRITIC*** uses an oracle to apply correction only to initially wrong answers. The latter must remain labeled an oracle, not a deployed detector. The same paper's code-execution and toxicity tasks study different reliability properties and cannot all be counted as factual-hallucination benchmarks.
- **Actual detector evidence.** Appendix D.1/Table 5 (pp.24–25) explicitly evaluates self-verification on **initial CoT answers**, using F1>0.6 against dataset answers to define correctness. It reports ACC/AUROC on the same QA splits as generation. On HotpotQA, Self-Eval AUROC is 0.713 and CRITIC is 0.831 under this setup. This is stronger evidence than merely naming a verifier, but does not test a fixed detector on both pre- and post-correction generated distributions.
- **Human/error analysis.** Appendix D.2.1/Table 6 (p.25) manually analyzes 100 sampled HotpotQA cases, including refusal, incorrect correction and label/matching errors. The discussion refers to error-sample proportions and categories; the denominator is insufficiently explicit for treating “10% incorrect correction” as a population-wide initial-correct→wrong rate. Appendix D.2.2/Table 7 (p.26) is clearer: in the 1,319-item GSM8K set, 363 initial errors become 246 remaining errors, while 41 originally correct answers are changed incorrectly. These are task-correctness transitions, not free-form factuality scores.
- **Preservation and evaluator reuse.** QA EM/F1 does not annotate all required aspects. Manual refusal/error categories give useful safeguards but are not a matched-coverage comparison. In QA, web evidence guides revision and dataset answers judge outcomes; the manual analysis exposes label limitations. In the separate toxicity task (Section 4.3, pp.8–9), Perspective API supplies both corrective feedback and final toxicity measurement, an explicit instance of scorer reuse. This does not imply the same reuse relationship in QA.
- **Resources and joint code.** Appendix A (p.21) discusses iteration-dependent inference overhead; Appendix D.3 (p.26) reports about 9GB cached web results from Jan–Apr 2023 and describes tool access as free. That statement concerns tools, not zero-cost LLM generation, elapsed time or API inference. We code **related component analysis**: detector discrimination, correction outcomes and manual transition/error analysis are present, without claiming a full post-intervention detector robustness audit.

## 8. SEAL

Primary source: [ACL 2025 paper](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.1199/). Read Sections 3–6/Limitations, pp.2–9, and Appendices A–F, pp.12–16.

- **Training and decoding are different from answer refusal.** Sections 3.2–3.3 (pp.3–4) shift part of the gold-token target mass to [REJ] according to the probability gap and add a regularizer. Main decoding penalizes trajectories with high [REJ] probability, while **forbidding [REJ] from being emitted**. Appendix E (p.15) confirms this. The Section 6 refusal extension (p.8) instead permits [REJ] generation, stops/discards the uncertain prefix and regenerates a refusal template. Main Table 1 gains therefore cannot be described as resulting from answer abstention.
- **Information control.** Table 1 (p.6) reports biography correct-claim counts with FActScore and LongFact precision/R@48/F1@48. Appendix C.2 (p.14) defines R@48 using truthful-fact quantity relative to 48; this differs from required-aspect coverage, and its printed definition should not be assumed identical to FactAlign's. Section 6/Figure 5 (p.8) separately plots known-question accuracy and unknown-question rejection across tau; Table 5 checks instruction following. These are meaningful refusal/utility safeguards, but not a single overall coverage-matched risk curve for all main generation tasks.
- **Uncertainty analysis and reference labels.** Section 6/Figure 4 (pp.7–8) uses 500 PopQA examples evenly split by proxy-model correctness, manually changed to cloze prompts, and inspects the probability of the *next* token being [REJ]. The known/unknown histograms test separation; they are not a reliability diagram or calibrated error probability for every free-form token/response. Appendix A.1 (p.12) uses correctness and Wikipedia popularity as knowledge proxies, not direct measurements of parametric knowledge.
- **Evaluation separation.** Appendix C (p.14) uses answer inclusion for several QA sets and Llama-3.1-70B for SimpleQA grading; biography uses Llama decomposition and retrieval+llama+npm; LongFact uses Llama decomposition with DeepSeek-V3 assessment. The scorer is distinct from the learned [REJ] signal, but generation/training/evaluation may still share corpus/model-family assumptions. The reported pilot correlation with GPT-4o has no numerical value in the inspected description; do not invent one.
- **Resources and attribution.** Section 4.4 (p.5) describes eight A100-80GB GPUs, BF16 and training budgets. Appendix E (p.15) sets **beam 8 for short QA, beam 6 for long QA**, versus greedy baselines. Section 5.2/Table 2 (p.6) removes both the rejection penalty and beam search when replacing decoding with greedy; this is not an equal-search-budget isolation of the penalty. Appendix F.3/Table 13 (p.16) varies beam size. Limitations (p.9) explicitly recognize overhead; measured equal-budget latency was not located.
- **Joint code and unresolved detail.** The [REJ] histograms, tuning/decoding ablations and refusal curves are **related component analysis**. They do not by themselves establish calibrated detection after the generation policy changes. The number of biography prompts conflicts between Table 8 (**138**, p.13) and Appendix B.2 (**183**, same page); retain this as unresolved rather than choosing a number silently.

## Cross-case synthesis permitted by this audit

These are case-grounded analytical conclusions, not prevalence estimates or newly measured performance effects.

1. **“Information control” has several incompatible meanings.** CAD's ROUGE, DoLa/TruthX's Info, CoVe's fact count, FactAlign's count-based recall and SEAL's correct-fact recall cannot be pooled. CoVe's within-study fact reduction and DoLa's truth/informativeness reversal show why the denominator must accompany the score. A prospectively information-matched comparison would need a separately defined target—answered questions, required aspects, or correct useful claims. No such new experiment has been performed here.
2. **Verification provenance is a graph, not a binary label.** FactAlign reuses its training/evaluation pipeline; CoVe hides the draft but reuses the generator; Self-RAG uses a learned self-reflection signal and has a bounded human audit; CRITIC's tool feedback differs from QA answer labels but its toxicity evaluator is reused. The audit supports documenting roles, inputs and denominator exclusions, not assigning independent/not-independent labels from model names.
3. **Resource evidence is heterogeneous but not absent.** DoLa reports measured latency/memory for a defined hardware and fixed-length protocol; FactAlign reports training time and API evaluation cost; CoVe gives a call-count formula; Self-RAG varies retrieval frequency; CRITIC records iteration/tool budgets; SEAL changes beam size as well as training. These accounts support access-specific comparisons, not a universal efficiency ranking.
4. **Relevant joint evidence already exists and must be credited.** Self-RAG's human output/reflection checks, CRITIC's self-verification discrimination plus error analysis, and SEAL's score separation plus refusal extension preclude blanket claims that mitigation work never checks verification. Their scopes differ from a full fixed-detector, before/after-intervention evaluation with stable human correctness units and matched useful coverage. That full protocol remains a proposed future design, not a result of this audit.
5. **Preserve the reference frame before combining methods.** CAD's deliberately counterfactual input-context test has a different success condition from DoLa/TruthX's world-knowledge tasks. Retrieval support remains bounded by the corpus. A system that improves supplied-source adherence need not improve world truth when the source is wrong.
