# Primary-text audit notes: five detection/evaluation cases

Verified: 5 October 2026. These are AI-assisted documentary extractions under `codebook.md`, not independent human double coding, replication, or a new benchmark. The five studies are the assigned subset of the fixed thirteen-study frame. The records establish what the inspected primary texts report; they do not establish field-wide prevalence. Author verification remains necessary before submission.

Only paraphrased audit notes and source metadata are included here. Downloaded PDFs and extracted text are kept outside the repository in `local_cache/acl_submission_sources`. One-based **PDF page** locators are used below; printed page numbers are supplied where available. The associated CSV contains all required fields. ## AlignScore

- **Target and mechanism.** Section 3.1 (PDF pp.2–3, printed 11329–11330) defines alignment of candidate information with context. Section 3.2, Eq.3 (PDF pp.4–5, especially 11332), pairs each candidate sentence with context chunks and averages the maximum alignment probability per sentence. Thus the final instance score is not a directly annotated token/span prediction or a count of independently verified world facts. It needs source/candidate text and its own trained auxiliary, not target-generator internals.
- **Metric evaluation and separation.** Section 4.2 (PDF pp.5–6) separates threshold tuning on validation sets from balanced accuracy on test sets and also reports AUROC. Tables 4/10/11 use human factual-consistency ratings for correlations. The TRUE average excluding PAWS, VitaminC and FEVER is explicit in §4.4.2/Table 3 (PDF p.7, 11334); those three datasets also appear in alignment training. This is not evidence that every evaluation is an unseen-dataset test. The paper does not equate model agreement with a new human factuality label.
- **Information boundary.** Appendix B.1 (PDF p.16, 11343) changes PolyTope's negative factuality coding to exclude Addition and Omission. In the paper's definitions these refer to irrelevant source content and missing key content, which need not be factual errors. This is a concrete separation of factuality from content selection, not a completeness-control experiment. The inspected §4/Appendix B evaluations assess scores on already generated outputs; generation usefulness after an intervention is outside that comparison.
- **Resources.** Appendix A.3/Table 6 (PDF p.15, 11342) reports model sizes, GPU types, epochs and **GPU Hour** values 100h/532h for base/large. Do not reinterpret them as measured per-query latency. No deployment-latency measurement was located in the inspected method, experiment and resource sections.
- **Interpretation limit.** The limitations (PDF p.10, 11337) explicitly discuss English-only coverage and synthetic-training errors that may not match natural generative errors. These support a bounded transfer concern, not a conclusion that the reported metric validation fails.

## FActScore

- **Reference frame must be explicit.** Section 3.1 (PDF p.3, 12078) expressly defines support relative to a user-trusted knowledge source in place of global truth. This audit codes **source-faithfulness to a designated external reference corpus**. It does **not** imply that the source was necessarily supplied to the generator. If a manuscript reserves “source faithfulness” exclusively for generator-input context, describe this case as “external-source-supported factual precision” rather than silently equating it with either input faithfulness or world truth.
- **Unit and conditional denominator.** The same definition averages atomic support fractions over responses and conditions the system expectation on responding. Tables 1 and 5 (PDF pp.4,8; 12079,12083) report responding fractions and fact quantity. Section 3.1 and Limitations (PDF p.10, 12085) acknowledge precision without recall. Counts expose information quantity, but neither counts nor a responding fraction establishes all required aspects were answered.
- **Human reference and estimator evaluation.** Section 3.3 and Appendix A.2–A.4 (PDF pp.4,14–15) describe human revision of automatic decompositions and source-support judgments. Section 4.2/Table 3 (PDF p.7, 12082) compares estimated and human system-level FActScores. Appendix B.2 (PDF p.17, 12092) separately measures atomic-fact classification assuming human-extracted facts. Those are different error budgets: classifier F1 with oracle decomposition is not whole-pipeline decomposition/verification accuracy.
- **Evidence restriction.** Appendix B.1 (PDF p.17, 12092) uses Wikipedia passages from the page corresponding to the person, top-five retrieval, and passages up to 256 tokens. Preserve this restriction when describing retrieval or generality. Unsupported can include missing support as well as contrary support.
- **Editing is an oracle-label analysis.** Appendix C's opening (PDF p.20, 12095) explicitly provides human-annotated FActScore labels to isolate editor performance from estimator errors. Sections C.1–C.3 (PDF pp.20–22) use ChatGPT with no-context/retrieval/atomic-label variants; the editing retrieval setting uses three passages rather than five because of context length. Section C.2 compares token preservation, newly added tokens and normalized semantic similarity with human-written edits. Table 14's ground-truth-label condition must accompany any claim about editing. These experiments support a related component analysis, not an automatic detector→editor result.
- **Resource scope.** The $15–25/hour and roughly $4/generation values in §3.3 describe human annotation. They are not API/runtime costs of the automatic estimator. A numerical all-in estimator deployment cost was not located in inspected §§4/B/C. The paper's differing hypothetical human-cost totals are not needed for this audit and are not used.

## SelfCheckGPT

- **Evidence versus labels.** Section 5 (PDF pp.3–5) compares a response sentence with additional samples from the target. Variants use embedding similarity, question answering, n-gram statistics, NLI or prompted judgments. Section 6/Fig.3 (PDF pp.5–6) labels factuality with human consultation of Wikipedia/Google; the detector's lack of external retrieval is distinct from how its reference labels were obtained.
- **Unit and evaluation.** The source contains 238 passages and 1908 sentences; 201 sentences have double annotation, and disagreements take the more severe label (§6, PDF p.5, 9008). Sections 7.1–7.2 distinguish sentence PR-AUC from correlations of aggregated passage scores. The original study's annotator-agreement results belong to that study, not to this AI-assisted audit.
- **Auxiliary access matters.** Section 7 (PDF p.6, 9009) uses twenty stochastic samples; target probability access is not needed for SelfCheckGPT itself. NLI needs a trained comparison model, and the prompting variant can use the same GPT-3 generator or ChatGPT. It is therefore incorrect to translate “black-box” into “no auxiliary model” or infer independent errors from a different comparator.
- **Information and joint scope.** Table 1 contains descriptive length statistics. Sections 7.1–7.3 and Appendix D (PDF pp.6–9,13–14) evaluate detection/ranking, comparator choices and sample counts. A content-completeness or output-release intervention test was not located there. This is a scope observation, not a defect imposed on a detector-only paper.
- **Cost is actually reported.** Appendix C (PDF p.13, 9016) estimates the Yes/No comparison stage over 1908 sentences and twenty samples at about $200 for GPT-3 or $20 for ChatGPT, using prices stated for the time of its experiments. Do not generalize this to current prices, generation plus detection total cost, or a per-question latency ranking.

## Semantic entropy

- **Keep protocols separate.** Methods (PDF pp.7–10) describe probability-weighted semantic entropy and a discrete estimator based on cluster frequencies. Sentence-length experiments use ten samples and GPT-3.5 entailment; reference passages are withheld from QA datasets. FactualBio instead decomposes GPT-4 biographies into claims, constructs six questions per claim, generates three new answers per question, includes the original claim, and averages entropy over questions. Its DeBERTa comparison uses at least one entailment direction with neither direction contradicting, a relaxed rule rather than the strict sentence-setting mutual-entailment test.
- **Target is not uniform world truth.** For sentence QA, GPT-4 judges whether a generated answer matches a dataset reference (Methods, PDF p.9). SI Note 6 (PDF pp.11–13, printed 51–53) explicitly says the human validation tests reference matching even when the reference itself is wrong. Math answers also make the evaluation a task-correctness proxy. In contrast, the FactualBio experiment manually checks 150 claims from 21 biographies (Methods, PDF pp.9–10). This motivates the CSV's mixed-frame code, not a blanket source-faithfulness or world-truth label for every experiment.
- **Human checks exist.** SI Note 2/Table 2 (PDF pp.3–4, 43–44) evaluates entailment against two human raters on 100 answer pairs for each of three datasets. SI Note 6/Table 4 checks reference-match judgments. These component checks and the manually verified biography claims must not be erased by a generic claim of “LLM-only self-evaluation.” Their exact sampled domains still limit generalization.
- **Coverage is explicitly tested.** Main Fig.2 (PDF p.3, 627), Fig.3 (PDF p.5, 629), Methods' metrics (PDF p.9), and SI Note 5/Fig.6 (PDF pp.10–12) evaluate accuracy among retained predictions and integrate over thresholds using AURAC. The primary selective-prediction experiment fixes the generated candidates and filters them using uncertainty. It therefore qualifies as a bounded generator/detector/selection composition test here, not as editing or deployment validation. Retained-question/fact fractions are not required-aspect completeness of an answer. AURAC also depends on base model accuracy, as the Methods explicitly notes.
- **Resources.** SI **Note 4** (PDF pp.7–10, 47–50) discusses pairwise comparison complexity, sample-count ablations, cluster reuse and caching. Some Methods cross-references refer to Note 3, but the supplied supplement labels the cost section Note 4. The audited locator follows the actual supplement. The main and ablation settings differ in entailment/checking models and sample count, so ablation costs must not be silently transferred to the main protocol. A complete measured runtime/dollar budget was not located in this inspected resource discussion.
- **Mechanism limitation.** The target is unstable confabulation; consistent misconceptions can evade meaning-dispersion scores. That is a mechanism boundary, not evidence that every incorrect answer is a confabulation.

## Lookback Lens

- **Mechanism and unit.** Section 2.1 (PDF pp.2–3, 1420–1421) computes per-head ratios from average attention over context and generated tokens, concatenates head/layer features, averages over a span, and applies logistic regression. Any hallucinated content makes an eight-token sliding window negative. This needs attention access and labeled data, not merely text or target token probabilities.
- **Actual joint experiment.** Section 3.1 (PDF p.4, 1422) uses the classifier to choose among sampled continuation chunks. Table 3 (PDF p.5, 1423) reports eight candidates per eight-token chunk and evaluates XSum, NQ and two MT-Bench outcomes. This directly tests the generator/selector combination; it is not merely detector AUROC on fixed baseline outputs.
- **Information safeguard and boundary.** Section 3.2/Table 3 distinguish GPT-4 original MT-Bench quality from GPT-4o contextual-faithfulness judging of fifty non-math items. NQ is scored by best-span exact match. These are concrete task-quality/correctness outcomes; they do not demonstrate matched required-aspect coverage in XSum or statistical equivalence of general quality.
- **Shared evaluation lineage.** Section 2.2 and Appendix B.1 (PDF pp.3,13; 1421,1431) use GPT-4o for training labels and reuse the annotation prompt for XSum evaluation. Appendix B.2 (PDF p.13, 1431) checks seventy XSum examples and fifty NQ examples with human judgments, reporting majority agreements of 68/70 and 47/50. This is a genuine check of the evaluator on those samples, but not proof of independent error patterns across training supervision, selection and final judging. The paper is more informative than a simple “same scorer”/“independent scorer” binary code.
- **Compute and annotation costs.** Appendix B.1 estimates GPT-4o costs of about $8 per 1000 XSum examples, $12 per 1000 CNN/DM examples, and $16 per 2655 NQ examples. Appendix C.1 (PDF pp.15,17; 1433,1435) reports A6000/V100 hardware, approximate guided-generation times of 20–30 seconds/example for 7B and 40–60 for 13B, and CPU classifier fitting. These estimates have their original workload scope and do not imply an all-method equal-budget ranking.
- **Transfer qualifier.** Section 4 (PDF p.6, 1424) and Appendix C.1 (PDF p.17, 1435) fit a linear mapping between 13B and 7B head features; 13B is run on the same 7B-generated text to obtain paired features. “Without retraining” refers to the original detector, not to absence of auxiliary fitting or access to the new model.

## Cross-case inferences supported by this bounded audit

1. **A shared word is not a shared target.** FActScore's designated-corpus support, AlignScore/Lookback's input-source consistency, SelfCheckGPT's human world-factuality labels, and semantic entropy's mixed reference-match/biography labels need distinct reference-frame descriptions. The source can be an input or an external evaluation authority; these are different roles.
2. **Information safeguards already exist, but measure different quantities.** FActScore reports responding fraction/fact quantity, semantic entropy evaluates retained prediction fractions, and Lookback Lens reports task quality alongside contextual faithfulness. None of those observations alone identifies semantic completeness or a fixed set of required answer aspects. No field-wide absence claim or proportion follows from these five purposively selected cases.
3. **Evaluation separation is a lineage description.** Human labels, human component checks, different comparison models, shared supervision and oracle information coexist within studies. The useful result is to identify those dependencies, not to assign an unsupported universal independent/not-independent flag.
4. **Component versus composition needs an intervention-specific definition.** FActScore's oracle-label editor analysis, semantic entropy's static selective-prediction curves, and Lookback's active candidate selection establish different facts. Only the latter two explicitly evaluate their own learned/constructed detector within the particular selection policy described. Neither automatically answers whether an unrelated detector remains calibrated after a different mitigation.
5. **Resource evidence is heterogeneous, not wholly missing.** AlignScore reports training compute, SelfCheckGPT historical checking expense, semantic entropy sampling/comparison structure, and Lookback measured approximate guided-generation time plus annotation cost. Preserve stage, unit and hardware; do not convert these into a universal efficiency ordering.

These are documentary findings. Any prospective matched-coverage, required-aspect or residual-error-calibration experiment proposed from them remains unperformed.
