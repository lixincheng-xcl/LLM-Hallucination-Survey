\section{Analysis and Future Directions}\label{sec:analysis}


\paragraph{Reference frames determine whether an intervention succeeds.}

A response can follow an incorrect document faithfully or contradict a document while being factually correct. Consequently, gains from CAD and attention-guided decoding answer a different question from gains on world-knowledge QA. We propose evaluating both source support and world correctness on the same examples, retaining an unknown label when evidence is insufficient. A controlled retrieval experiment should vary document correctness and order while keeping questions and the generator fixed. Astute RAG motivates the conflict axis; Stable-RAG specifically addresses sensitivity to retrieval permutations \citep{wang-etal-2025-astute,zhang-etal-2026-stable}. Measuring both axes would distinguish stronger evidence use from more reliable evidence selection.

\paragraph{Confidence and internal signals are useful proxies, not truth certificates.}

Repeated agreement and confident internal activation can arise from a stable false association. \citet{cheang-etal-2026-llms} distinguish associated from unassociated hallucinations and find that the former resemble factual recall in the internal representations they study. This constrains broad interpretations of successful probing, uncertainty alignment, or representation editing: a detector may identify missing knowledge more readily than confidently recalled misinformation. Future evaluations should partition naturally generated errors by these mechanisms, hold out generator families, and test whether external verification improves detection specifically on confident falsehoods. The claim is not that internal methods cannot work, but that average detection accuracy conceals which failure process they recognize.

\paragraph{Metric improvements must preserve useful information.}

Factual precision does not reward answering the difficult parts of a question. Controlled long-form experiments show that response length affects measured precision \citep{zhao-etal-2025-response}, while ICAT explicitly measures the coverage of expected information aspects \citep{samarinas-etal-2025-beyond}. A mitigation comparison should therefore report unique supported claims, aspect coverage, refusal rate, and latency in addition to precision. For selective systems, the risk--coverage curve compares the error rate among retained answers at each answered-question fraction. For revision, the analogous quantities are corrected errors, newly introduced errors, and retained supported claims. Matched-length analyses can identify whether an apparent gain comes mainly from producing less information.

\paragraph{Evaluation must remain independent of optimization.}

\citet{godbole-jia-2025-verify} find that attribution evaluators with similar balanced accuracy can disagree on individual examples, trade false positives against false negatives differently, and misestimate system-level error rates. They also identify sensitivity to paraphrasing and evidence spread across distant document regions. Optimizing or selecting answers with one verifier and then evaluating with that same verifier therefore risks rewarding its preferences. A stronger protocol uses independent human auditing, reports class-specific errors, and tests on held-out domains and generator families. Benchmark timestamps also matter: \citet{jiang-etal-2026-benchmarks} show that outdated reference facts can distort evaluation. Versioned evidence and periodic label review should accompany longitudinal comparisons. These requirements complete the evaluation loop linking Figure~\ref{fig:mitigation} to Figure~\ref{fig:evaluation} and Table~\ref{tab:hallucination-benchmarks}: an intervention is useful when independently verified improvements persist under matched evidence, useful coverage, and realistic computational budgets.

\paragraph{Limits of the synthesis.}
The evidence base favors English text tasks and ACL-family research. Model access, generators, prompts, judges, and budgets differ across studies; reported improvements are not a controlled ranking. Selected full-text checks support the mechanisms discussed in detail, while the broader catalogue has shallower screening. The proposed tests remain future work. Appendix~\ref{app:comparisons} records complementary evidence and its boundaries, and Appendix~\ref{app:protocol} makes the evaluation recommendations explicit.
